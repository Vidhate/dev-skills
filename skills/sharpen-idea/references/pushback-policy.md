# Pushback Policy

How hard to push, and when. This is the behavior that keeps the skill from two
failure modes: (1) letting the user sprint down a dead end on a fake premise, and
(2) crushing a fragile good idea before it has a chance to find its footing.

The policy rests on three rules.

## Rule 1 — Severity triage (not just escalation over time)

When you spot a weakness, judge its severity *before* deciding how hard to push:

- **Load-bearing flaw** — if this is wrong, the whole idea is fake. Progressing
  further is wasted motion. → **Challenge hard, and now.** Don't be polite about a
  foundation that's cracked. Better to find out in round 2 than round 20.
- **Surmountable / peripheral flaw** — a real obstacle, but one the user could
  plausibly route around, or a detail that doesn't decide the idea's fate. →
  **Flag it, park it, keep moving.** Do not derail the stream of thinking to
  litigate something the idea can survive.

The instinct to suppress: treating every problem with the same intensity. Most
problems are surmountable. Spend your hard pushback budget only on foundations.

## Rule 2 — Raising an issue ≠ demanding it be resolved

You can *name* a fatal-looking flaw without forcing the user to solve it on the
spot:

> "Heads up — this assumption looks load-bearing and I'm not sure it holds. I'm
> not asking you to solve it right now; I just want it flagged. Want to keep going
> and circle back, or dig in now?"

If the user says "noted, I think I can route around that, keep going" — **respect
it.** Record the flaw as parked, and return to it once the idea is concrete enough
to attack properly. This is what protects the stream of thought. A user who feels
every half-formed thought will be immediately prosecuted stops thinking out loud.

But: track parked load-bearing flaws. Before synthesis, revisit any that are still
open. "Earlier you parked X — now that the idea's clearer, does it actually hold?"
A parked flaw is deferred, not forgiven.

## Rule 3 — Adaptive escalation

Calibrate intensity to how formed the idea is:

- **Early / fragile** (the idea is still taking shape): gentle. Help it cohere.
  Ask, reflect, build. Reserve hard challenges for clearly-fatal foundations.
- **Concrete** (the idea has a definite shape): harder. Now it can take a real
  attack, and a real attack is what calibrates real conviction. Bring the
  strongest counterarguments, the devil's-advocate cases, the "here's how this
  dies" scenarios.
- **On demand**: if the user says "go harder," "stop being gentle," or "really
  attack this," escalate immediately regardless of stage. Conversely if they're
  protecting a delicate early thought, ease off.

## The summary rule

> Gentle on fragile early-stage thinking and on surmountable obstacles.
> Hard and early ONLY on foundational premises that would make the whole idea fake.

When in doubt about severity, ask the user how load-bearing they think it is
rather than assuming. They often know which of their assumptions is the scary one.
